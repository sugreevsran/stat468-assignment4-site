I wanted to answer what players are most efficient in goal scoring. This would be done by looking at which players score the most goals per second of ice time. This will help to produce players who can score with limited ice time, which can be used by coaches in situations where a goal is needed quickly.

To achieve this, I first used pandas to create a new column in the dataframe called goals per 60. This measures goals scored per 60,000 seconds of ice time and will be my metric of efficiency going forward.

df["goals_per_60"] = df["goals"] / df["icetime"] * 6000

I then filtered out players who played less than 5,000 seconds of ice time to avoid ratios that were overinflated from fluky players.

df_active = df[df["icetime"] > 5000]

Lastly, I sorted the results in descending order to find the most efficient goal scored when you consider time on ice.

result = df_active.sort_values("goals_per_60", ascending=False)

The results showed that Leon Draisaitl, David Pastarnak and Brandon Hagel are the top 3 players in the league in terms of goals per 60. This means that they score the most efficiently when taking time on ice into account. It’s important to note that some elite scorers may not appear here due to their heavier usage in non-scoring roles, such as penalty killing. For example, Auston Matthews is widely considered as the best goal scorer in the league, but he does not show up on this list because he is also used on the penalty kill, where goals forwarded are uncommon.

import pandas as pd
from lets_plot import *

1. Visualize

In hockey, especially in the playoffs, shutting down opposing lines can be just as valuable as scoring goals. I wanted to evaluate which players are the best at suppressing the quality and quantity of chances against them. To do this, I built a scatterplot that compares two defensive metrics: expected goals against (xGA) and Corsi against.

  • Corsi Against: This measures the total shot attempts (on-goal, missed, blocked) directed at the net while a player is on the ice. If Corsi Against is low, that means a player is limiting the raw shot volume of a team.

  • Expected Goals Against (xGA): This weights each shot attempt by the likelihood of it becoming a goal. Lower xGA against means a player is limiting the other team’s high-danger scoring chances.

The following is the code snippet I used to construct the plot:

skaters2 = pd.read_csv("skaters.csv")

division_map2 = {
    'TOR': 'Atlantic', 'TBL': 'Atlantic', 'FLA': 'Atlantic',
    'WSH': 'Metropolitan', 'CAR': 'Metropolitan', 'NJD': 'Metropolitan',
    'WPG': 'Central', 'DAL': 'Central', 'COL': 'Central',
    'VAN': 'Pacific', 'EDM': 'Pacific', 'LAK': 'Pacific'
}

skaters2['division'] = skaters2['team'].map(division_map2)

fwds2 = skaters2.query("position in ['L','C','R'] and team in @division_map2")

tot2 = (
    fwds2
    .groupby(['name', 'team', 'division'], as_index=False)
    .agg(
        icetime=('icetime', 'sum'),
        xGA=('xGoalsAgainstAfterShifts', 'sum'),
        corsiA=('corsiAgainstAfterShifts', 'sum')
    )
)

tot2['xGA60'] = tot2['xGA'] / (tot2['icetime']/3600)
tot2['corsi60'] = tot2['corsiA'] / (tot2['icetime']/3600)

top3_2 = (
    tot2
    .sort_values(['team', 'icetime'], ascending=[True, False])
    .groupby('team', as_index=False)
    .head(3)
)

highlight2 = top3_2.query("name == 'Mitch Marner'", engine='python')

ggplot(top3_2, aes(
    x='xGA60', y='corsi60',
    color='team', shape='division'
)) + \
    geom_point(size=4) + \
    geom_point(data=highlight2, size=6) + \
    geom_text(
        data=highlight2,
        mapping=aes(label='name'),
        hjust=0, vjust=0,
        color='black'
    ) + \
    labs(
        title='Defensive Profiles of Top Ice-Time Forwards',
        subtitle='Expected Goals Against vs. Corsi Against (per Hour)',
        caption='Data: skaters.csv',
        x='Expected Goals Against per Hour',
        y='Corsi Against per Hour',
        color='Team',
        shape='Division'
    ) + \
    theme(
        axis_title_x=element_text(color='blue', face='bold'),
        axis_title_y=element_text(color='blue', face='bold')
    )

Interpretation

Every dot is one of the top-3 forwards (in terms of icetime) for each of the 12 best teams in the league (I chose the top 3 teams from each division in terms of points). The colour will tell you the team and the shape will tell you the division.

Most players are going to be grouped near the middle of the chart. This will be around 0.03 xGA per hour and 0.75 Corsi Against per hour. This will be the average of the most used forwards on the best teams in the league. Players who are furthest from the origin will be weaker defensively by either allowing too many shot attempts against or too many high-danger chances against. The most notable outlier is Nikita Kucherov, who is the pink cross on the upper right of the plot. He’s a player well-known for being elite offensively, but not someone who can be relied on in the defensive zone.

Players who have low xGA/h but high Corsi Against/h are likely players who allow their opponents into their defensive zone but will force them to take shots from bad angles (known as the perimeter). Conversely, players with low Corsi Against/h but high xGA/h can be a little misleading. These players are good at limiting shots, but they allow high danger chances more often. This could mean that they have poor defensive structure and are easier to exploit by the other team’s top players.

The closer a player is to the origin of the chart, the better they are defensively. These will be players who do not allow shots and whenever they do, they are not dangerous. These players are the ones who will get nominated for Selke trophies. From this plot, it is clear that Mitch Marner is one of those players. He sits exactly where you would want a shutdown forward to be, and that lines up since he has already been nominated for a Selke before and is often praised for his defensive abilities.

df3 = pd.read_csv("skaters.csv")
